Transforming how a government service is provided online

Summary: how I re-designed a service's digital experience

I re-organised Dorset's adult social care services website so it was based on user needs at different stages in people's journeys. I carried out primary research and pair-wrote to make the case for change and for a robust design.

How I found out about service users' needs

Dorset Council wanted to better help people and families needing care online. So I asked to listen to contact centre calls, to learn about service user needs. Dorset Council had no user researchers and my desktop research only told me about existing service users.

I found out from call listening that many people avoided contacting us until their care needs were great. Those who looked at our site found it difficult to understand what could help.

I saw an opportunity to make it easier for people to access activities, advice, and equipment.

How I found out what content was useful and was missing

To discover how we might reorganise content, I audited content, mapping this to the user needs I'd uncovered.

I found patterns in the journeys of people into the care system, including 'denying needing help,' experiencing a trigger, and a resulting 'new life situation'.

I placed content into these journey sections and saw that at some stages, content was overwhelming; at others, there were content gaps.

How I designed content to reflect people's needs

I convinced the heads of the care service by sharing how we were both over- and under-providing information.

I set out a plan for re-designing, removing, or adding to content in new sections dedicated to different journey stages. The heads of service asked the relevant subject experts to work with me to develop the content.

I arranged to pair-write content without source information. This reduced back-and-forth and I published the new site before my 1-year contract ended.
